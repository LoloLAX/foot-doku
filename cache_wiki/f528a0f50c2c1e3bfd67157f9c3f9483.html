Endrick Felipe Moreira de Sousa Pessoa (born 21 July 2006), known as Endrick (Brazilian Portuguese: [ˈẽ(j̃)dɾik(i)]),[3] is a Brazilian professional footballer who plays as a striker for Ligue 1 club Lyon, on loan from La Liga club Real Madrid, and the Brazil national team.

Early life

Endrick was born in Taguatinga, Federal District, and has one brother and two sisters.[4][5] His father, Douglas de Sousa Silva Ramos, pursued a career in professional football and left his family when Endrick was eleven years old to play for several small clubs in Brasília.[5] His mother was unemployed and homeless during that time, forcing Endrick and his siblings to reside in an orphanage in São Paulo until his father returned after six months.[5] Both his parents worked at a cafe on the city's underground train station before his father received employment by Palmeiras as a janitor.[6][7]

Endrick started playing football at the age of four,[2] and his father published his goals on YouTube to attract interested parties among the big Brazilian clubs.[8] Endrick vowed to become a professional footballer to help his family after his parents were unable to feed him.[6][7] At the age of eight, he trained in a Real Madrid camp with his friends at Águas Claras; he grew up supporting Real Madrid and idolised Cristiano Ronaldo.[9] He was also inspired by other Brazilians who played for Madrid such as Ronaldo, Vinícius Jr., Rodrygo and Éder Militão.[9]

Club career

Palmeiras

After nearly signing for São Paulo, Endrick joined the Palmeiras youth team at the age of 11.[6] In the next five years, he would score 165 goals in 169 games for Palmeiras youth teams.[8] Endrick participated in the 2022 Copa São Paulo de Futebol Júnior, where he scored eight goals in seven matches and was voted Player of the Tournament by supporters, after leading Palmeiras to their first title.[10][11] Following the tournament, he caught the attention of international media and several major European clubs.[12][13][14]

Endrick made his senior professional debut on 6 October 2022, coming on as a second-half substitute in Palmeiras' 4–0 league win over Coritiba.[15] At the age of 16 years, two months and 16 days, he became the youngest player to appear for the Palmeiras first team.[16] He scored his first two goals on 25 October, in a 3–1 win over Athletico Paranaense, becoming the second youngest goalscorer in the history of Brazil's Série A.[17] Endrick then achieved his first trophy with the first team, winning the league with three games in hand following a 4–0 home win over Fortaleza on 2 November. He scored his third goal in seven appearances during the match as Palmeiras secured an eleventh league title in club history.[18] At the end of the season, Endrick was voted the most promising player in the Série A.[19]

On 11 October 2023, Endrick was named by English newspaper The Guardian as one of the best talents born in 2006 worldwide.[20] After experiencing an extensive goal-drought throughout the season,[21] Endrick scored two goals and added an assist on 1 November in a 3-4 comeback win against Botafogo at the Estádio Olímpico Nilton Santos. The game was 3-0 at the end of the first half. Endrick scored early in the second half and was shown on screen telling his teammates "just pass me the ball", a phrase he'd later apologize for. With the win, Palmeiras moved three points behind Botafogo, who were then first in the table.[22][23] Palmeiras won their second consecutive league title, after Endrick scored a goal in a 1–1 draw to Cruzeiro on 6 December, two points above second-place Grêmio.[24][25] He finished the season with eleven goals, the second highest scored in a Serie A season by an under-18 player behind Ronaldo.[26]

On 16 May 2024, Endrick was forced to leave the field on a stretcher due to an apparent right leg injury during Palmeiras' clash against Independiente del Valle in a Copa Libertadores group stage match, and he was subject to further medical tests.[27]

Real Madrid

On 15 December 2022, La Liga club Real Madrid announced that they had reached an agreement with Palmeiras, Endrick, and his family to sign him in July 2024, when he turned 18.[28] The official signing fee was undisclosed by the clubs, but was reported as an extendable three-year deal for a fixed amount of €60 million plus €12 million in taxes.[29] However, some other sources reported the fee was fixed at €35 million, with the remaining €25 million subject to achieving specific sporting objectives.[30] On 19 July 2024, Real Madrid confirmed that Endrick would sign a six-year deal.[31] He was unveiled as a new player on 27 July at the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium.[32]

On 25 August, Endrick made his debut in a 3–0 home win against Real Valladolid in La Liga, scoring the third goal after his introduction as a substitute in the 86th-minute.[33] He also became the youngest foreigner to score for the club in La Liga's history (aged 18 years and 35 days) surpassing Raphaël Varane, who scored against Rayo Vallecano aged 18 years and 152 days.[34] On 17 September, Endrick scored on his UEFA Champions League debut when he scored the final goal in 3–1 win against VfB Stuttgart in the league phase, becoming the youngest player to score for Real Madrid in the competition and also becoming the youngest Brazilian footballer to score in the competition, aged 18 years and 58 days.[35][36] On 2 April, Endrick became the first player since Cristiano Ronaldo in the 2012–13 season to score five goals in a single Copa del Rey campaign for Real Madrid, later finishing as the tournament's joint second top scorer. He opened the scoring in the semi-final first leg against Real Sociedad and added his fifth with a trademark chipped finish in the return leg, helping Madrid reach the final.[37]

On 21 May, Real Madrid’s medical services diagnosed Endrick with a right hamstring-tendon injury that would keep him out for at least two months, ruling him out of the FIFA Club World Cup.[38] In August 2025, he acquired the number 9 shirt ahead of the 2025–26 season, his new number being vacated by Kylian Mbappé changing to the number 10.[39] On 1 November 2025, he made his first appearance under new manager Xabi Alonso, in a 4–0 win against Valencia.[40][41]

Loan to Lyon

On 23 December 2025, Endrick joined Ligue 1 club Lyon on a six-month loan deal. Lyon paid a loan fee of €1 million plus half of his wages.[42] On 11 January 2026, he made his debut, scoring a goal in a 2–1 away win over Lille in the Coupe de France.[43] On 25 January, he scored his first goals in the league, a hat-trick in a 5–2 win over Metz.[44]

International career

Youth career

On 18 March 2022, Endrick was called up to the Brazilian national under-17 team to compete in the 2022 edition of the Montaigu Tournament in France.[45] With five goals in four matches, including one against Argentina in the final, Endrick finished as the top scorer and was named best player of the tournament, as Brazil won their first Montaigu Tournament title since 1984.[46]

Endrick was initially included in Brazil's under-20 squad for the 2023 South American Youth Championship in Colombia. However, as his club Palmeiras elected not to release him, he would ultimately not be a part of Brazil's title-winning team; Palmeiras did not want Endrick to miss the start of their pre-season, which overlapped with the tournament's January and February timeframe.[47]

Endrick was selected for the Brazil national under-23 team in 2024.[48] The team failed to qualify for 2024 Summer Olympics in Paris, France, with him scoring twice in seven matches in Olympic qualifying.[49][50][51]

Senior career

Endrick was selected for the Brazil national team for the first time on 6 November 2023, to participate in the 2026 World Cup qualifiers against Colombia and Argentina.[52][53] He became the youngest male player to secure a senior call-up since Ronaldo in 1994.[54] On his debut against Colombia, he became the fourth all-time youngest player, and youngest in 57 years, to make his debut for Brazil.[55][56][57] His side fell to a 2–1 defeat at the Estadio Metropolitano Roberto Meléndez as Colombia achieved their first qualifier victory over Brazil in their history.[58]

Endrick scored his first goal for Brazil in a friendly against England at Wembley Stadium on 23 March 2024, making him the youngest player to score for club or country at the ground.[59] He scored his second goal for his nation three days later in a 3–3 draw to Spain.[60] On 10 May, he was called up for Brazil to participate in the 2024 Copa América.[61] After three sub appearances in the group stage, he made his first start for Brazil, replacing Vinícius Júnior who was suspended, in a quarter-final defeat to Uruguay.[62] Endrick completed one pass during the match, from kick-off, as his side lost 4–2 in the penalty shoot-out.[63] On 18 May 2026, Endrick was named to Brazil's 26-man squad for the 2026 FIFA World Cup.[64]

Personal life

After scoring the winning goal for Brazil over England on 23 March 2024,[59] Endrick revealed that he idolised the late English forward Bobby Charlton. Brazil and Real Madrid forward Rodrygo revealed on 8 September 2024 that his teammates began calling Endrick "Bobby" as a form of banter after he revealed his admiration for Charlton.[65]

In September 2024, Endrick married his girlfriend, Gabriely Miranda.[66][67]

In November 2024, he became a Baptist Christian and was baptized at Lagoinha Baptist Church in Madrid.[68]

Style of play

A left-footed forward with a good shot, Endrick is considered one of Brazil's best football prospects,[69][70][71] and has drawn comparisons with legendary Brazilian strikers Ronaldo, Pelé, and Romário.[72][73] Endrick has rejected comparisons of his ability to Brazilian legends and particularly towards Pelé.[74] When asked to describe his style of play, Endrick said, "I'll always fight. I'll be persistent and try until the last minute I'm in the game. I never give up, I pressure defenders, I run more than anyone else on the pitch."[75] His father has stated that the footballer has been inspired by Portuguese forward Cristiano Ronaldo.[76]
