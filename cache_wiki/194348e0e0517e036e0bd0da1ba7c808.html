Zakaria El Ouahdi (Moroccan Arabic: زكرياء الواحدي;[1] born 31 December 2001), is a professional footballer who plays as a right winger or a right-back for Belgian Pro League club Genk. Born in Belgium, he plays for the Morocco national team.

Early life

[edit]

El Ouahdi was born in Hoboken in the Antwerp Province in a family of eight children. His parents are of Moroccan descent.[2]

Club career

[edit]

In June 2021, El Ouadhi joined the under-21 side of RWD Molenbeek.[3] After good performances, El Ouadhi was regularly invited to train with the first team, and was awarded his first professional contract on 9 September 2021.[4]

On 14 August 2021, he made his professional debut in Challenger Pro League in RWD Molenbeek's 3–1 victory against Royal Excel Mouscron.[citation needed] On 3 October, he scored his first goal in professional, awarding a 2–1 win for his club against Deinze.[citation needed]

On 12 March 2023, El Ouadhi scored his first career hattrick against Club NXT, offering an important 3–2 win for RWD Molenbeek for their fight for promotion.[5][6] He terminated the 2022–23 season by winning the league with RWD Molenbeek and gained a promotion to the Jupiler Pro League.[7][8] El Ouadhi named was as Molenbeek's player of the season and figured in the league's best XI of the season.[9]

On 5 September 2023, El Ouahdi signed for Genk on an initial three-year deal with the option for a further twelve months.[10]

International career

[edit]

Holding both Belgian and Moroccan citizenship, El Ouadhi chose to represent Morocco in international level. On 9 June 2023, he was called up by coach Issame Charaï in Morocco U-23 squad for the 2023 U-23 Africa Cup of Nations.[11] El Ouadhi appeared in four out of five matches during the tournament, and scored one goal against Mali U-23 in the semi-final match.[12] Morocco U-23 won the tournament as El Ouadhi figured in the competition's team of the tournament as the best right-back.[13][14]

El Ouadhi earned his first senior call-up for 2025 Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers against Gabon and Lesotho in September 2024, but remained on the bench for both games.[15][16]
