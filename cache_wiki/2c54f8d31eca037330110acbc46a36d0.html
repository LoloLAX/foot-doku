Álvaro Fidalgo Fernández (born 9 April 1997) is a professional footballer who plays as a midfielder for La Liga club Real Betis. Born in Spain, he represents the Mexico national team.

Club career

[edit]

Real Madrid

[edit]

Born in Hevia, Siero, Asturias, Fidalgo joined Real Madrid's La Fábrica in 2012, from Sporting de Gijón.[2][3] On 21 June 2016, after finishing his formation, he was loaned to Segunda División B side CF Rayo Majadahonda for the season.[4]

Fidalgo made his senior debut on 20 August 2016, starting in a 0–0 draw against Sestao River Club. In July 2017, after being a regular starter, he returned to his parent club and was assigned to the reserves also in the third division.

Fidalgo made his first team debut for Real Madrid on 6 December 2018, coming on as a late substitute for Vinícius Júnior in a 6–1 home routing of UD Melilla, for the season's Copa del Rey.[5][6] For the 2019–20 season, he was named team captain of Raúl's Castilla.

Castellón

[edit]

On 25 August 2020, Fidalgo signed a three-year deal with Segunda División newcomers CD Castellón.[7] He made his professional debut on 12 September, coming on as a second-half substitute for Jorge Fernández in a 2–1 away win against SD Ponferradina.[8]

Club América

[edit]

On 1 February 2021, despite featuring regularly at Castellón, Fidalgo joined Mexican club América on a season-long loan.[9] On 2 June 2021, Club América confirmed the permanent signing of Fidalgo. During his time with the club, he helped the team secure three league championships.[10]

Betis

[edit]

On 1 February 2026, Fidalgo returned to Spain to join Real Betis.[11]

International career

[edit]

In February 2026, FIFA approved Fidalgo’s request to switch his international allegiance to Mexico, having previously represented Spain at youth level.[12] The following month, he received his first call-up and made his senior debut on 28 March in a friendly against Portugal.[13]

Personal life

[edit]

Fidalgo's grandfather Rafael was also a footballer. A defender, he played professionally for UP Langreo, Real Oviedo and Caudal Deportivo.[2] He gained Mexican citizenship after spending more than 5 years in the country.[14]
