Abdul Fatawu Issahaku (born 8 March 2004), commonly known as Abdul Fatawu, is a Ghanaian professional footballer who plays as a winger for EFL League One club Leicester City and the Ghana national team.

Club career

[edit]

Steadfast

[edit]

In 2019, at the age of fifteen, Fatawu started his career with Tamale-based club Steadfast FC, in the Zone One of the Division One League, the Ghanaian second tier. During his debut season, he immediately established himself as one of the most talented players in the league, scoring eight goals and assisting five in 13 matches before the league was halted due to the COVID-19 pandemic.[3]

In his second season, he scored 12 goals and made 12 assists in 14 league matches and picked up 8 man of the match awards.[4][non-primary source needed] In 2021, after the Africa U-20 Cup of Nations, Fatawu was linked with several European teams, some of them being Liverpool, Bayer Leverkusen, and Sporting CP.[5][6] In October 2021, Fatawu joined Dreams on a loan until 2022.[7] In the same month, he was included in The Guardian's "Next Generation 2021", and was described by them as "arguably the best African prospect of his generation".[8]

Sporting CP

[edit]

In April 2022, he was signed by Sporting CP on a five-year contract with a release clause of €60 million.[9][10] This brought finality to rumours of him joining Sporting CP on loan from Liverpool and other transfer saga rumour.[6] Prior to signing officially Fatawu had been at the club since February and had been training with the U-23 side. As he was an under age player at the time, and with the season already ongoing, he couldn't be registered with the first-team until the 2022–23 season, then, he began to train with the B team until available to play for the professional team.[11][12]

Ahead of the 2022–23 season, Fatawu was named in the squad for pre-season training and tour for the first team.[13] He was involved in the team's preparations prior to the start of the season playing his first pre-season game against Belgium side Royale Union.[14] On 7 August 2022, he was named on the bench for the first match of the season. The following weekend he made his debut after coming on in the 80th minute for Francisco Trincão in Sporting CP's 3–0 victory over Rio Ave.[15]

Loan to Leicester City

[edit]

On 31 August 2023, Fatawu signed for EFL Championship side Leicester City on a season-long loan from Sporting CP.[16] He made his club debut in the EFL Championship three days later, coming on as a 54th-minute substitute in a 1–0 home loss to Hull City.[17] On 21 October, he scored his first goal for the club in a 3–1 away win against Swansea City.[18] On 23 April 2024, he scored his first professional hat-trick, in a 5–0 home win against promotion rivals, Southampton.[19] There was a clause inserted into the loan that required Leicester to purchase Fatawu upon promotion for €17 million, which they did in May 2024. 50% of the difference between the sale price to Sporting (€1.2 million) and Leicester was provided to Fatawu's original club, Steadfast FC, who had inserted a sell-on clause in his sale to Sporting.[20]

Leicester City

[edit]

On 1 July 2024, Leicester City activated the buy clause and signed for €10 million on a permanent five-year deal contract with €7 million additional fee.[21] As a result, his jersey number changed from No. 18 to No. 7.[22] On 19 August 2024, he made his first Premier League debut as part of the first team squad for Leicester City against Tottenham Hotspur. In that same game, Fatawu provided his first Premier League assist for Jamie Vardy in a 1–1 home draw.[23][24] On 13 December 2025, he scored from his own half in a 3–1 victory over Ipswich Town.[25]

International career

[edit]

Youth team

[edit]

In 2018, Fatawu was part of Ghana's national under-17 team managed by Karim Zito.[26] The team reached the finals of the 2018 WAFU Zone B U-17 Cup of Nations. They however lost in the final to rivals Nigeria via a 3–1 penalty shootout after a 1–1 draw after regulation time, with Fatawu missing his penalty in the shootout.[27][28] Fatawu remained a member of the Ghana U-17 team and served as captain of the team in 2020, playing during the 2021 Africa U-17 Cup of Nations qualifiers, during 2020 WAFU Zone B U-17 Cup of Nations.[29][30] At the tournament he scored the equalising penalty in Ghana's 1–1 draw against Nigeria.[31][32] Ghana were eliminated from the competition after losing 3–1 against Ivory Coast in their final group game. Ghana's only goal of the game was assisted by Fatawu.[33]

The following year, due to his impressive form, Fatawu was promoted into the under-20 squad at the age of 16, despite being much younger than the majority of his teammates. He was later selected to take part in the 2021 Africa U-20 Cup of Nations.[34] During the competition, he played all of the Black Satellites' matches, scoring two goals in the group stages to help his side win the title for the fourth time in its history.[35][36] In that occasion, he was also adjudged as the Best Player of the Tournament.[37][38]

Senior team

[edit]

In March 2021, after his exploits with the U-20 side, Fatawu was given his first call-up to the senior team by C.K. Akonnor, as the Black Stars challenged South Africa and São Tomé and Príncipe in two qualifying matches for the Africa Cup of Nations.[39] He was called up alongside teammates from the U-20 AFCON winning team, Philomon Baffour and Ibrahim Danlad.[40]

Fatawu scored a free-kick goal against Uzbekistan when the Black Stars' B Team played Uzbekistan at the Markaziy Stadium. Even though the game ended 2–1 for Uzbekistan,[41][42] he was highly praised by his performance.[43][44][45]

In September 2021, Fatawu made his debut for the Black Stars in a 3–1 victory over Zimbabwe in a 2022 FIFA World Cup qualifier.[46][47] Fatawu made the 28-man squad for 2021 Africa Cup of Nations.[48] He was the youngest player in the Ghanaian team and the fourth youngest at the tournament.[49] On 27 September 2021, Fatawu scored his debut goal for the Ghana senior team against Nicaragua.[50]

On June 2, 2026, Fatawu was integrated by Ghana's coach Carlos Queiroz into his list of 26 players in order to compete in the 2026 FIFA World Cup.[51]
